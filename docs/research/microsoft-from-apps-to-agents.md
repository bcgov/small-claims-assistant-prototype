# Microsoft: From apps to agents

Source: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/blog/2026/03/12/from-apps-to-agents-rearchitecting-enterprise-work-around-intent/

## What the article says

Microsoft argues that enterprise software is shifting from app navigation to intent-first orchestration. The user states the goal. The agent figures out how to carry it out.

The article says apps are not disappearing. Their role is changing. They become trusted services that agents invoke, not destinations people have to navigate through.

## Main takeaways

- Intent is becoming the primary interaction layer.
- Apps should expose trusted capabilities, not every UI action.
- Agents should orchestrate work across systems, rules, and permissions.
- Shared control planes matter once many agents exist.
- Governance, lifecycle management, and observability are part of the platform, not extras.

## Why it matters here

This is a direct match for the BC Gov front-door idea.

The vision here is not just “replace forms with chat.” It is:

- route by intent and domain first
- let the specialist agent handle the work
- keep applications as trusted systems of record
- use a control plane for security, identity, and governance

That applies to forms, but it also applies to government knowledge brokerage. A citizen can ask for a service form, a policy answer, or a domain-specific explanation, and the front door should route them to the right expert either way.
