What about the other side of the coin with Microsoft's Frontier Tuning (Build 2026)?

A few days ago I wrote about how Frontier Tuning allows organizations to train custom AI models on their own workflows and potentially delivering up to 10x efficiency gains while keeping everything inside their secure environment. (https://lnkd.in/eM_8iixB)

The upside is compelling. Imagine tuning an AI to strictly follow verified tax rules and procedures. For agencies like the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), where studies have shown error rates around 83% in citizen responses, this kind of workflow-trained agent could meaningfully improve accuracy, reduce response times, and increase overall capacity.

But we should also be clear-eyed about what it actually takes to make this work.

HOW THE AI LEARNS YOUR WORKFLOWS
Frontier Tuning relies on a reinforcement learning loop (the "RL Gym"). To get meaningful results, the system needs structured examples of good performance:

• Upfront effort: Creating training and validation datasets (JSONL format) based on real workflow histories like how top performers handle cases, make decisions, write responses, and manage exceptions.
• Ongoing improvement: The system benefits from production traces (including tool calls, successful outcomes, and corrections) to keep "hill-climbing" and getting better.

This creates a data flywheel: the more the AI is used, the more it can learn from real activity.

THE GOVERNANCE QUESTIONS WE NEED TO ASK
This approach raises legitimate questions that leaders should not gloss over:

• Does effective use of this technology require ongoing logging of employee workflows and decisions?
• How do we maintain employee trust while capturing the data needed for continuous improvement?
• Could this capability be used to monitor individual performance, plan workforce changes, or accelerate automation of certain roles?

Employees would essentially be helping train the system that may, over time, take over parts of their current work.

None of this is inherently malicious; many organizations already log activity for compliance and security reasons. However, turning behavioral traces into training data for AI is a significant step. It demands thoughtful data governance, clear policies on consent and transparency, privacy impact assessments, and honest conversations with employees about how work will evolve.

BOTTOM LINE
Frontier Tuning is real innovation that can deliver better service and efficiency, especially in high-volume, rules-based environments. 

BUT success requires more than technology. It requires mature leadership that treats this as a WORKFORCE AND PROCESS TRANSFORMATION, not just an AI project.

Organizations that get this right will be transparent, invest in reskilling, and use productivity gains to improve service, rather than just reducing headcount.
